## Question

Prerequisite: [What battlefield layout creates competing demands on commander position?](07-battlefield-layout.md).

For the agreed mirrored two-route battlefield, what starting forces, reinforcement group sizes and cadence, and broad battlefield scale create competing fights without overwhelming the player?

Decide where the commander and initial soldiers begin, how successive reinforcement groups activate the routes, and what qualitative relationship between route separation, influence radius, and travel time makes relocation matter. Choose a prototype starting configuration rather than a final balance prescription; exact combat statistics and numerical tuning can remain adjustable.

Test whether both routes can remain relevant, whether fresh reinforcements can contest a threatened home area, and whether the commander can react to a new threat without trivially supporting both fights. Account for the roughly ten-minute battle target, automatic enemy stance switching, and the shared cross-passage.

## Comments

### Agreed starting encounter configuration

The user agreed to all four recommendations:

- Both sides begin with six soldiers each, split three per route, inside their home defense area and ready to advance. Both armies start in Attack. The commander starts near the player base between the route entrances.
- Each side receives three soldiers every 20 seconds. Successive groups alternate routes; both sides use the same timing and first route. Each route receives fresh soldiers every 40 seconds, and reinforcements pass through their home defense area.
- Initial travel targets are roughly 8–12 seconds for the commander to change routes through the central cross-passage and 15–20 seconds to return from a midfield fight to the player base. Usual fighting positions remain too far apart for one stationary commander to influence both.
- There is initially no hard soldier population cap. Combat and reinforcement timing should produce small local fights of roughly 3–8 soldiers per side. If prolonged Defend or other conditions create unreadable crowds, revisit reinforcement cadence or a cap during prototype tuning.

These are adjustable prototype starting values, not final balance prescriptions. The user subsequently confirmed the complete configuration.

## Answer

The user confirmed the following prototype starting configuration for the mirrored two-route battlefield:

- **Starting forces:** each side begins with six soldiers inside its home defense area, split three per route and ready to advance. Both armies begin in Attack. The commander begins near the player base, between the route entrances, allowing an initial choice of which advance to support.
- **Reinforcements:** each side receives three soldiers every 20 seconds, with successive groups alternating routes. Both sides use the same timing and first route, so each route receives fresh soldiers every 40 seconds. Reinforcements pass through their home defense area before advancing and can intercept home threats under the existing local-defense rules.
- **Battlefield scale:** initial commander travel targets are roughly 8–12 seconds to switch routes through the central cross-passage and 15–20 seconds to return from a midfield fight to the player base. Usual fighting positions remain too far apart for one stationary commander to influence both. Travel consumes meaningful battle time while leaving promptly noticed threats answerable.
- **Population:** initially impose no hard soldier population cap. Aim for small local fights of roughly 3–8 soldiers per side, with numbers emerging from combat and reinforcement timing. Prolonged Defend is a specific accumulation scenario to examine; revisit reinforcement cadence or a population cap if crowds become unreadable.

These quantities and travel times are adjustable prototype starting values, not a final balance prescription. They establish competing initial fights and recurring route pressure without guaranteeing a roughly ten-minute battle or a particular combat outcome. Exact combat statistics, influence tuning, and other numerical rules remain for a subsequent decision and prototype evaluation.
